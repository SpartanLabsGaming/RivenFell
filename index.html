The Magic System

The elaborate mechanical core of the world: how mages are put together, how they cast, how they fight, and the rules that bind it all.

Foundational Laws

The bedrock rules every school of magic answers to.

READ THIS FIRST

This set of notes is called the Language, Notation, Disambiguation,  and Discussion Pages. Refer here when uncertainty regarding terminology or other related topics arises

Concept: The Laws of Magic

1st Age Law: The only actions impossible to perform with magic are those inconceivable by the caster.

2nd Age Law: Magic has a cause and an effect. The cause is the energy put in and the conditions met, the effect is the spell. The stronger the cause the greater the effect.

3rd Age Law: World Destructive Magic is prohibited. Ex: time travel, teleportation, divination, fabrication

4th Age Law: Provisions are to be made for non-destructive use of WDM.

The First Generation Magic Classification System

The first generation magic classification system is the first system that attempts to define specific schools of magic and find a place in a classification tree where they belong.

This system separates schools primarily on their apparent nature and classifies them on the rules which govern (or don't) that school.

First of all the first gen system defines all magic to be either within or outside the Magic Spectrum which is a set of a few generic laws which govern the majority of conceivable schools of magic.

Those magic schools that fall within the Magic Spectrum are referred to as Spectres. They are split into 3 Tiers which are defined by the rough difficulty of the spell cast from the perspective of the mage's skill.

Those magic schools that do not fall within the Magic Spectrum are referred to based on the subsection to which they belong. These subsection are defined by how far they stray from the Magic Spectrum.

When speaking about any school of magic defined by the first generation system the matter of whether the school does or does not fall withing the spectrum is not directly stated. It is left to be deduced that a school does fall within the spectrum when it is referred to as a Spectre or spoken of as belonging to a Tier of the spectrum.

Generation 1: The Magic Spectrum

The Magic Spectrum is a model meant to define a standard spell. It was conceived and is used by the First Generation Magic Classification System. The requirements for a school to fall under the Magic Spectrum: The spell must have a standard invocation, use mana as a power source, and spells within it must have MSM efficiency improvement as they are practiced.

Known deferents of the Magic Spectrum:

1. The Indefinite Spell Deferent subsection includes spells that do not fall under the spectrum. However these either do not have enough common ground with other spells or are not of high enough quantity to be defined under their own school of magic. Ex. secondary cast spells.

2. The Restriction Deferent subsection covers spell that are restricted. Restricted spells are not practiced and therefore their casting conditions are unknown.

3. The Provision Deferent subsection covers provisions. The Provisions at specific placed on a MSM chart do not improve with practice and therefore they do not fall under the Magic Spectrum.

4. The Nested Spell Deferent subsection covers spell that do not use mana as their power source. These include spell that use mana for means other than power.

5. The Niche Spell Deferent subsection covers spell that do not use any energy value as a power source. These include schools the spells in which use a user skill threshold and systems the values of which are not energy values such as the reach and retraction system.

Differences

Tier: a rough estimation of the complexity of the schools of magic that fall into it.

School: Spells similar in their magical nature but not in their effects. Ex: charter, jitsu, shout

Greater school: a school of magic that spans the tiers and has subschools in separate tiers.

Class: A Magically predefined set of abilities that share a unique trait such as a key or form  Ex: Vampire, Lycanthrope, Ninja

Style: a user defined set of abilities to be used as a way to fight. Ex: Healing, Stalling, Sacrifice.

Aspect: a non-predefined set of abilities that cross schools of magic but achieves similar purposes in their effects. Ex: Body modification, Cloning

Spectre: a school of magic that exist inside the magical spectrum, follows its rules, and uses shock ex: Charter spells, Elemental spells, Natural magic, Abstracts

Nest: a school of magic that is outside of the spectrum and still follows its rules, but uses an energy other than shock. Ex: juitsu and chakra, kidou and spiritual pressure,

Niche: a school of magic that exists outside the spectrum, does not follow its rules, does not use energy, and improves through the mage's use of and insight into it and specific abstract niche-defined ways ex: Shouting, demonizing, fullbring, blessings/curses

Concept: Restrictions and Provisions

Some fields of magic are considered to bring too much harm to a dimension to allow humanoid magic weilders to use. These fields of magic are referred to as restricted magic.

Fields of restricted magic include but are not limited to: time control, teleportation, divination, weaving.

While complete power over these fields may not be permitted, there are still ways of practicing these fields without breaking any rules. This is done through Provisions.

Provisions are specific ways of performing restricted magic that are allowed. These include but are not limited to: blink type spells (for instant teleportatio), 2nd gen prepara type spells (for long distance teleportation), limited time control within a defined space (for time control), 2nd gen prepara + 2nd Gen hard polycrea requirements for divination

Note: When speaking of Restrictions the word restriction is not used to mean that a restricted spell is possible but prohibited. A restricted spell cannot possible be cast by a humanoid or a non-diety entity.

The Mage: Anatomy & Energies

What a mage is made of, and the energies that flow through them.

Concept: Mage (Components)

Rift seed: The part of a mage that is the centerpoint of the mage's connection with the magical dimesions. Through it shock flows from the dimensions into the mage. And through it mana is converted into spell momentum during spellcasting. Its potency determines the rate of duplication of life into shock.

Soulstone: the part that contains the mage's soul. When full the soul is whole when empty the mage is dead. As the mage gains expertise in the art of magic he becomes able to increase the capacity of his soulstone by sacrificing some of his soul. The soulstone regenerates soul over time until it is filled. If a mage's soulstone is not destroyed with the mage's body the mage's soul can continue to live withing the soulstone, however it will not regenerate without a body.

Transcension orb: resides within a person's brain and contains their mind. It is responsible for mind battles between humans and mages alike, however mages are able to reinforce theirs with shock. Its strength decides the potency of mind invasion and defense, the mage's awareness, and castpeeking. It feeds off of soul. Both a mage's soulstone and transcension orb are required to recreate their body.

Eldunari: originally unique to dragons it was the center of creation of life energy, if can be artificially created by mages and even humans. Eldunaris in mages will continuously provide the owner with life regardless of their intake of food. Eldunaris in humans will provide the owner with superhuman endurance and vigor and allow them to resist spells with sheer lifeforce.

The body: the mage's body is his source of life energy which when created gets duplicated by the rift seed into shock. This causes mages to pursue more muscle mass as well as magically reinforcing their body using a technique called elding which makes the natural body be more fit and produce more life energy. The physical strength and speed of a mage could also prove to be a decisive factor during a fight.

Concept: Mage (Energies)

Health: the current condition of the mage's physical body

Life: the total energy available within a mage's physical body

Vigor: life that is readily available to be used for physical tasks

Shock: a mage's magical energy

Mana: magical energy readily available for casting

Soul: the content and condition of the mage's soul

Concept: Energy Flow (Natural)

Soul: regenerated slowly by the soulstone until the soulstone is filled. Regeneration speed is based on the maximum capacity of the soulstone.

Life energy is harnessed as the body digests food. This can be amplified with a magical technique called elding. Life can also come from an authentic or artificial eldunari.

When life energy is originally created from the mage's body or from an internal artificial eldunari it is duplicated by the rift seed to provide the mage with shock. The amount of shock gained is proportinal to the life energy processed but is modified by a coefficient which represents the mage's power.

Concept: Energy Flow (Controlled)

A mage has control over the flow of energies within self. The control is limited to conversion of one type of energy to another and change of the rate of conversion; there does not exists the ability to willfully create or destroy energy.

1. The Healing conversion: the most well know and the most commonly used converion. It requires both an energy cost and an energy to be used for conversion. It takes spell momentum as a cost for converting life into health. Each part of this conversion may be affected by the mages potency: amount of spell momenta produced, SM cost for conversion, life produced from conversion, rate of conversion, etc.

2. Persistence, the life force of battle. The second most common conversion is one of the things which allow a mage to persist in a fight once out of mana. The conversion uses the life force as a cost for speeding up the conversion of shock to mana.

3. Soul Rip, the last resort. It is the act of ripping ones own soul and offering it to an alternate dimension self in exchange for life force and a temporary increase in shock duplication rate.

Amping

To begin to use magic the mage has to first set their rift seed into a ready (amped) state which begins the flow of magical energies within the mage. The amped state requires maintenance payed in shock directly from the shock core. The intensity of the amped state can be increased to further increase the speed of the flow of energies within the mage but this also increases the maintenance cost. Amp levels increases are done in rigid set amounts called gates rather than small and fluid increases. Some advanced spells have a certain set amp gate that is a requirement for them to be cast. Other advanced spells do not officially have an amp gate requirement but might require a certain energy flow speed, setting a de facto amp gate requirement.

Example: goku going super saiyan is going through his next amp gate.

Concept: Units

Spell momentum: effective energy within a cast spell.

Spell power: the amount of momentum being put out by a spell over a period of time (at an energy cost)

Spell force: momentum being put out over a period of time at no cost. (With soul, artefact/relic, cracle, spirit, or dragunity)

Casting & Channeling

The moment-to-moment mechanics of putting a spell into the world.

Concept: Casting spells (Energy)

The caster starts concentrating the flow of magic on a certain channel point in his body through which he will channel mana into a spell at a speed based on on his mana pull. First the mana is channeled and built up into an empty spell shell, then when the caster believes he has enough mana he then invokes the spell and converts the mana in the spell shell into spell inertia. The base amount of spell inertia per mana channeled is based on the strength of the spell and can vary based on the skill of the caster. Some spells either require the caster to channel them through a certain channel point or perform better when cast through it. The caster's skill with specific channel points can vary.

Casting Spells (Channeling)

Channeling is when a caster has to actively and consistently put energy into an ongoing spell and control its flow.

Backcasting is the ability to supply and control chaneled spells in the back of one's mind. The caster has to be significantly more advanced than the spell requires to backchannel it. Casters with advanced minds can backchannel several spells at once.

Frontcasting is putting the majority of one's focus into the control of a channeled spell. If the focus is broken the channel will most likely be interrupted.

Concept: Reach and Retraction

As the caster casts a spell he must REACH into the depths of his mind and access his magic. The are 3 types of reach: magical reach, branch reach, and spell reach. A spell has a spell reach and a branch reach requirement both of which must be met in order to cast the spell. Most branches also have a magical reach requirement which must be reached for the caster to be able tomuse the branch. When a spell is cast, the caster's reach RETRACTS by an amount based on the spells RETRACTION value. The more proefficient a caster becomes the more quickly he recovers his reach and the higher his maximum reach values can get. The more practice and experience the caster has with a spell or element the lower are the spell's or element's reach requirement and retraction.

Concept: Castpeeking

Cast peeking is the practice of constantly backchanneling a spell upon another mage in order to gain information about their outgoing magic, be it an actively cast, channeled, or already activated spell. Cast peeking protection can also be cast upon ones self in order to decrease the extent to which the cast peeking mage gains information. A very high strength or power cast peek can lead to the ability to cancel the cast of a spell in its tracks.

Combat Mechanics

How spells are costed, defended against, and broken.

Critical Strike

Note: nothing to do with the traditional critical strike which is a chance to do bonus damage upon striking the enemy.

Step 1: Tempering

Tempering is the use of either the blacksmithing or enchanting schools of magic to enable a weapon to temporarily store a limited amount or spell momentum. Tempering is more effective when done through blacksmithing than enchanting. Enchanted tempering is temporary.

Step 2: Stacking

Stacking is withholding a proportion of spell momentum that was to be used upon striking the enemy and instead storing it within a tempered weapon.

Step 3: Critting

Critting is the release of spell momenta stored within a tempered weapon all in one devastating strike.

The best use of critical strike is keeping the tempered weapon stacked without critting, forcing the enemy to use up energy to defend against a critical strike that might or might not come.

Deposition

Deposition is the study of placing idle spell momentum into the world.

Loading: the act act of providing SC spell with spell momentum to be used on cast time.

Lacing: the act of placing spell momentum on a moving object for it to be used as kinetic energy on impact

     Sharping: weapon lacing

     Tai: body lacing

Magic poison: the act of leaving spell momentum inside a person's body after having gotten through the IMP to serve a purpose such as negating healing.

Mechanic: Spell Defense

These are the steps that can be done in defense from a spell. The earlier the level the earlier it takes place, the more castpeeking it requires, and the less energy is used relative to the offensive spell.

1.Cast-blocking: done when the defending mage has top-level castpeeking.The defending mage has such extensive castpeeking that he is able to stop the very cast of a spell.

2.Diffusing: Stops a spell from forming spell momenta by casting its true reverse momentarily knows through castpeeking.

3.Counter casting: done by traditionally casting a counterspell. Spell momenta is formed, deployed, and destroyed.

Dodging and parrying: avoiding the spell rather than confronting it.

4.Shielding: spell momenta is formed and deployed. A shielding spell is cast to absorb it.

5.Deffecting: deployed spell momenta is starting to take effect, counter-effect spell is used to prevent the effects from taking place.

6.Undoing: the effect has been done recently however a magical trace is left for a short time. It is used to undo exactly the effects of the spell.

7.Healing: effects are not undone but amends are made.

Mechanic: Damage Types

Physical: reduces health, reduced by armor

Magical: reduces health, reduced by absorption, living resistance

Brutality: reduces maximum health

Bleed: periodic damage to health based on bleeding intensity, additional bleed damage increases bleeding intensity, damage to health grows exponentially as bleeding intensity increases

Heat: increases temperature , damage to health, maximum health, and stats as well as various negative effects past tolerable temperature thresholds

Cold: decreases temperature, damage to health, maximum health, and stats as well as various negative effects past tolerable temperature thresholds

Lightning: damages health and vigor as a percentage of health burned, increases if hits a target wearing armor without being blocked, leaves a dot based on the amount of armor the target is wearing unless the effect is negated completely, the dot can bounce to nearby targets

Spectral: percentage of magical damage dealt additionally to soul

Pierce: physical damage that ignores armor

Ethereal: percentage of physical damage dealt additionally to health while ignoring physical damage reduction

Absolute: unreduceable (not unavoidable) damage to any value

Poison: flat dot with a time limit, additional poison damage increases duration

Disease: eats away at life and grows if unattended

Venom: stat inhibitor that increases in strength over the course of its duration

Toxin: decreases health, life, and inhibits natural recuperative properties of the body

Negating the effect of special damage types does not heal the damage done by them.

Effects of bleed, temperature changes, and toxin naturally decrease over time.

Mechanic: Spell Cost Calculation

This refers exclusively to the mana cost of a spell. The parallel or alternate costs of a spell are not necessarily subject to this mechanic. 

In general the mana cost of a spell depends on the spell itself and the modifications made to it by the caster. The amount of mana required to modify the spell depends on the degree to which it is being modified as well as the type of modification that is being made. There may also be limits put on the variability of the power of a spell, and the increase in the modification level might not scale the same way as does the cost.

The mana cost of a modification to a spell is called a cost factor. A cost factor belongs to one of four categories based on how it affects the cost: valley cost factor, hill cost factor, trough cost factor, and cliff cost factor. There may be any amount of cost affecting factors to a spell, as little as one or over twenty. There is no rule regrading how many of the factors have to be of a certain type, they may be evenly distributed, there may not be any factors of a certain type, or they may all be of the same type.

The valley type mana cost affecting factor:

A valley type factor is flat and unchanging. The power put into a mana cost factor of this type cannot be modified and therefore its effect on the mana cost of the spell is constant.

The hill type mana cost affecting factor:

A hill cost type factor means that the factor's effect on the mana cost of the spell is directly proportional on the amount of power put into the factor. By default the mana cost scales linearly with the power, however there are exceptions. For example if the mana cost increases parabolicaly with the power the factor can then also be referred to as a mountain type mana cost affecting factor.

The trough type mana cost affecting factor:

A trough type mana cost affecting factor provides the best power to mana cost efficiency at a defined point on the relationship. Decreases in power from that point will still yield a spell that costs less mana and visa versa. However, straying from the defined point in either direction will decrease efficiency.

The cliff type mana cost affecting modifier:

some spells are able to perform forbidden magic by using built in provisions. When the amount of power put into these provisions reaches a specific threshold there is an additional cost to using them. The cost increases exponentially with power. The threshold is just a regular relevancy threshold and can be increased with experience in casting the spell, mage power, equipment, and threshold specific mage values and modifiers.

Mechanic: Level/Experience

Rather than keeping two several called level and experience instead there will only be a dynamically updated level value. Unlike all the previously known level systems this level value will not be rigidly bound to integer values and will be allowed to be a decimal. This means that the level value could, for example, be 2.4 if the mage is 40% through level 2. This will allow all things whose efficienciesnare dependent on the level to have their efficiencies updated every time experience is gained rather than every time the level goes up an integer value. However this also means that the difficulty of levelling up will increase as dynamically as the efficiency benefits. For example the same amount of experience gained will increase the level value more if it is 2.2 than if it is 2.6.

addExp (double expGained){

   level += expGained / expNeededToIncreaseByOneLevel (level);

}

Study: Nuking

Definition

Nuking is the act of selecting an AOE to be "nuked" by one's shock in order to have spells in that AOE negated. Shock that is used for nuking is drawn directly from the shock core and is not converted to mana or spell momentum. Mana cost increases with AOE size. The spell negation is the most generic type so therefore when a mage chooses to nuke they forfeit any possible optimization/purification that would come with a more specialized counter.

Purpose

There are 3 most common uses for nuking.

1. There is a high level of urgency on negating a spell and there is not enough time for cast peeking/defense.

2. The caster is very unfamiliar with a spell and castpeeeking/defense would be too difficult.

3. The caster is low on mana and/or has excess shock and therefore the loss of shock is a relatively small price. (It is always an absolutely large price as it is the highest possible price that can be paid for a counter.)

Wraith Control

The Ability to cause the mage's own wraiths (projections, shadows, clones, etc.) to inherit the casters innate characteristics and temporary buffs.

Advanced Studies

Rarer disciplines mastered only by the deeply committed.

Study: Hyping

Hyping is the act of infusing one's body with shock to havenit readily available for use. The infused shock cannot be put back into the shock core and eventually expires and becomes electricity. Hyping is usually done prior to battle for if it is not used then it is wasted. The primary use for infused shock is healing, this way shock does not have to be directed towards the hurt body part, it is already there, and the mage has to neither dedicate channeling space towards healing nor use up mana from his mana pool.

Other uses for hyping: hyped shock can substitute some of the vigor used for an action, can be consumed to increase the consumption of vigor to increase speed of movement, battletime warwalking, and body oriented spells such as thorns.

Study: Inception

The magical dimension exist within the same space as the physical dimension and so our body exists and is made of magic on the magical planes. Inception is the study of the magical makeup of the mages own body and mind and the practice of using it for our means and modifying it at will.

Requieres high mentality and mage power. Requires practice with tier 3 magics and body modification magics. Allows access to unique inner powers as well as innate (non-class) true forms.

Study: Lichdom

Not be confused with the Lich class. Lichdom is the practice of merging ones own ody with magic gaining the ability of using energies of the body as a magical resourse. To perform lichdom the mage extends the rift seed to take physical presence within the mage's own body.

Stringing is the practice of finishing a spellcast that a mage has insuffiecient mana for by using vigor. The amount of vigor needed to finish the spell increases exponentially as the amount of mana required to finish the cast increases.

Grasping is the ability to retain ones current reach during the cast of a spell which would require retraction. The technique is channeled and in most cases can also be backchanneled, vigor is consumed for the duration of the channel.

Gazing is a frontchannel-only technique used to rapidly increase ones reach within one branch. Life and shock are consumed alongside mana.

Compression is the practice of using life to decrease the manacost of spells. Life cost increases as the manacost increases. The factor by which life cost increases is a root of the factor by which manacost increases. The root is based on lichdom strength, the starting point is based on mage strength.

Study: Purging

As a mage increases in arcane power more and more shocks starts to be produced withing their body, however, the capacity for the amount of shock that can be contained within the mage's body does not necessarily increase at the same rate. If the maximum shock capacity is reached and there is still shock being produced without any outgoing shock, shock starts to overflow and seep out of the mage's body in the form of free magic. Purging is the practice of creating an outlet for all the extra shock being produced as well as using the otherwise lost shock to one's advantage.

Study: Transcention

Transcention is the act of breaking the barriers between different areas of magic and merging into one whole part that combines and superseeds the combined areas.

Miasm(Magical) is a magical contortion created through transcention, where multiple elemntal bases are used as one in elemntal casting.

Ascention(Magical) is the act of creating a custom class that is a result of combining accessible classes through transcention.

Aspects & Techniques

Cross-cutting practices that don't belong to a single school.

Aspect: Body Modification

Alteration: the practice of modifying ones own body as one sees fit.

Shapeshifting: the practice of taking on the form of a human, humanoid, animal, monster, abomination, etc. It is not the use of illusion magic to make one's self appear different.

Elemental fusion: making an element a part of yourself.

Druidism: a more advanced form of shapeshifting in which the mages soul modifies itself making it more like that of the new form for the duration of the shapeshift in order to gain more of the form's abilities.

Elding: the practice of infusing one's own body with magic in orded to improve physical performance as well as shock duplication rate. (Ex: Eragon's transformation to an elf)

Truemorphing: the act of changing ones current form to an alterante one of your own. (Ex: vampires' monster and bat forms)

Demonizing: the act of inviting a spectral being (usually an "english demon") into one's own body in order to improve physical performance, receive or upgrade generic demon powers, or get the demons own powers.

Riftwalking (lichdom): the practice of fusing ones body with magic itself.

Great walk: the act of taking upon one's self the form that one's alternate self from another dimension (physical or magical) owns. (Ex: Terrorblade's perfect self)

Aspect: Clones

Most of the aspect of clones is not directly provided for by the schools of magic. However mages have come up with useful ways of making copies of themselves through magic. They are listed in order from least powerful to most powerful.

6. Illusion clones

These are not real. They are not actually copies of the mage and they do not exist. The are created as illusions that look like the mage only to confuse the opponent.

5. Dummy clones

Useless dummies used mostly as meat shields to take damage from attacks. Could also be used effectively as illusions (they are not actually illusions, they do physically exist and therefore will not be dispelled by anti illusion magic, possibly convincing opponents that they are real.)

4. Soft clones

Often much weaker than the original this is the first level of cloning spells that creates clones that can actually do things and actively participate in battle. They are mostly likely much easier to destroy than the original and there is a "real" one which if killed stops supplying magical energy needed to maintain the clones.

3. Sonido clones

Not actual clones, these are copies of the mage created by extremely fast movement making it seem as if there are several of the mage. They are all the same one and are therefore all "real".

2. True clones

Actual full strength copies of one's self. They draw power from the same source, which is not duplicated, but have independent energy flows. The flow of each separate clone is smaller than that of the original but their combined flows are greater than that of the original. They are the original split into pieces and if destroyed the last one standing becomes the "real" one.

1. Planeswalker clones

Alternate versions of the mage himself from the magical dimensions. Every one is every bit as powerful as the original with their own independent energy flows and reserves equal to those of the original. They can be dispelled using spells from the "Banishing" school of magic. There is a real one which is easily distinguishable from the rest and if killed severs the existence of the clones.

Painting

Painting is the act of infusing a spell with the essence of an element.

The spell is not required to be either elemental or non-elemental.

Painting an elemental spell with the essence of another element will cause the spell to have properties of both elements.

The magnitude of the effect of the properties gained through painting is based off of both the power of the original spell and the intensity of the paint.

Painting requires a mana cost of its own that is based on the painting spell and not on the painted spell, this can be used to increase the MTSM efficiency of a spell.

The bottlecaps for painting is often secondary tree reach as all painting spells are within it.

Healing

The Tier 1 school of recuperative magic, in full.

Generation 1: Tier 1: Healing

Healing is a Tier 1 school of magic as defined by the first generation magic classification system. The Healing school of magic falls within the Magic Spectrum. Healing is defined as counteracting damage to a living body. There are several ways in which this can take place: Healing: Terminology. Although healing might require life energy to be conducted it is still considered to fall under the magic spectrum, this is further discussed in Healing: The MS Discussion.

Healing: Terminology

This discusses the terminology for recuperative magic.

Healing: the process of decreasing the negative effects on the physical body.

Recovery: the body's natural process of  consuming life energy to increase health.

Restoration: the use of shock to increase the speed of recovery.

Regeneration: a quick, unnatural health increase by magical means.

Reconstruction: increasing maximum health by recovering from brutality damage

Skinweaving: the act of creating flesh to recover from physical-based damage, brutality, and maim.

Instantaneous Regeneration: The ability to produce a burst of healing magic (usually of multiple types) strong enough to instantly replace lost flesh.

Toxin Threshold: an amount of toxin effect on a mage at which the passive healing abilities are decreased to the point at which they are (effectively) disabled and cannot even remove the toxin effect itself forcing the mage to actively cast healing magic to combat the toxin

Healing: The MS Discussion

The Magic Spectrum is an important part of The First Generation Magic Classification System which has requirements for schools of magic to fall under it. One of these requirements is to have mana be the primary power source for the spells within the school. This discusses why Tier 1: Healing in fact, meets this requirement.

Although some spells within the Healing school of magic use up life energy alongside mana, particularly in the restoration category (see Healing: Terminology), the life energy is not being used as a power source. It can instead be thought of as a material that is the target of the spell. When energy is being put towards the casting of a healing spell, (whether through Invocation or Channeling), none of that energy is life energy. Only when the spell actually starts to take effect does life energy becomes needed as it is being used to heal the target.

A good way to understand this process is to think of healing as of a factory. The Healing School is the factory, the spell is one of the machines in the factory, mana is the energy powering the machine,  life is what the machine is working on, and health is what the machine produces.

As the Magic Spectrum only cares about the energy input of a spell (mana) but not the conditional input (life) the Healing School of magic qualifies to be in the spectrum.

Generation 1: Tier 2: Elding

Elding, not to be confused with skinweaving, is an advanced magical technique used to reinforce the mage's physical body through magical means. Some of its potential benefits are: more vigor, tougher/more resistant body, higher production of life energy, superhuman bodily functions and abilities. Elding spells may bring some or all of the aforementioned benefits to the mage and may also vary depending on whether they affect the whole body or only specific body parts. Elding spells are unique in that they are one time only cost and effect spells. Meaning that the caster only has to cast it once to permanently gain the benefits of the spell, however elding spells can only be cast either once or until elding is done to a certain extent past which they will have no efect.

Phenomena

Things that happen to magic, rather than things magic does.

Phenomenon: Free Magic

Magic with a will of its own that springs up around the casting of some spells. May cost the caster some shock. Improperly executed spells create a rift in the dimensional fabric and allow magic from other dimensions to seep in. A spell can be improperly executed if 1. the caster is not skillful enough to perfectly cast the spell, in which case the caster has some of the mana used for the spell become free magic, 2. The caster is not powerful enough for it in which case the power needed to coplete the spell will not be supplied and the spell will be only partially cast, however the magic rift created by the spellcast will not be smaller which will allow some free magic to seep out. 3. The spell was created poorly thus rendering it corrupt. 4. The cast of a spell suffered from loose spell momentum making it lose grasp on the proper space in the magical dimension.

Phenomenon: Rift Vacuum

Aka Cracle effect. As a mage increases in power, his rift seed eventually reaches a point at which its influence on the physical world starts spreading beyond the mages body. This phenomenon is called the rift vacuum. A rift vaccum is a constantly present rift in the fabric between the physical and magical dimensions that is letting free magic seep into the physical world. This scales with the mage's active amp gate and ceases to exists at amp gate 0. The free magic behaves just like free magic from similar phenomena such as those which occur when spells are miscast. It is harmful to those within the aoe but harmless to the mage responsible for the rift. The free magic released by this phenomenon can be manipulated to either avoid hurting specific target or made into spells, most often from the singe, buffing, auras, and awareness schools of magic. Ex: (of rift vacuum and natural harm only) spiritual pressure from bleach.

Generation 1: Tier 1: Granting

Granting is a Tier 1 school of magic as defined by the first generation classification system. Granting is a spell that "grants" the target the ability to perform other spells.

Requirements: knowledge of the granting spell, a live target who will be using the grant(humanoid or entity). A de facto requirement is the grantee's knowledge of the granted spell, otherwise the granting spell will still be cast but the grantee will not be able to use it.

Notes:

1. The granted spell is not required to be either an existing castable spell nor a nonexisting one.

2. The granted spell is also not required to be charted. A natural spell can be conceived by the mage during the cast of the granting spell and become the grant. Such a granting spell is referred to a free grant. After the creating of a free grant, natural spells given by the grant cannot be modified.

3. A grant spell works entirely independently of any source of power to it. It is up to the mage to provide a power source for the grant.

4. A grant is not limited by the numeber of spells it can provide.

5. A granted spell is a secondary cast spell and therefore does not require invocation.
